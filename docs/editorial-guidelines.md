# Date Night Ideas Editorial Guidelines

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Version | 1.0 |
| Updated | 2026-07-19 |
| Applies to | Hub, Picker, results, category pages, Spokes, and Idea pages |

## 1. Editorial Goal

Help a visitor stop browsing and choose a date they can actually do. Copy should create desire, remove uncertainty, and make the next step feel easy.

The voice is warm, observant, specific, and lightly playful. It should sound like a thoughtful friend who knows the constraints, not a content generator describing a category.

## 2. Writing Principles

1. **Lead with a human tension or payoff.** Start from what the couple wants tonight: reconnect, laugh, get out of a rut, spend less, or avoid complicated preparation.
2. **Make the scene visible.** Name concrete actions, objects, timing, or choices instead of relying on adjectives.
3. **Give the user a decision.** Prefer a clear recommendation over a long menu of possibilities.
4. **Earn every claim.** Explain why an idea fits the selected budget, time, location, relationship stage, or vibe.
5. **Keep it attainable.** Do not make ordinary users feel that a good date requires luxury, perfect decor, or elaborate planning.
6. **Use natural American English.** Contractions are welcome. Sentence length should vary. Read every published paragraph aloud.
7. **Add editorial value.** Source material provides research input; our copy must add structure, execution detail, constraints, useful variations, or a sharper point of view.

## 3. Avoid AI-Sounding Copy

Do not use filler openings or generic claims such as:

- "Whether you're looking to..."
- "Looking for the perfect way to..."
- "This delightful activity is sure to..."
- "Create unforgettable memories"
- "Elevate your date night"
- "Perfect for couples of all kinds"
- "Embark on a journey"
- "There's something for everyone"

Also avoid repeated sentence templates, adjective stacks, excessive exclamation marks, vague romance language, and conclusions that merely restate the title.

## 4. Idea Page Pattern

Each Idea should contain:

1. **Title:** concrete, distinctive, and easy to picture.
2. **Hook:** one or two sentences that sell the moment or solve the user's tension.
3. **Why it fits:** a specific explanation tied to actual Picker answers.
4. **How to do it:** three to five short, executable steps.
5. **At a glance:** realistic time, budget, location, preparation, and relevant limits.
6. **Make it yours:** one useful variation, conversation prompt, scoring rule, or small twist.
7. **Fallback:** where relevant, a lower-cost, lower-energy, indoor, or alcohol-free variation.

## 5. Examples

Weak:

> Elevate your evening with a delightful DIY pizza experience that is perfect for creating unforgettable memories together.

Better:

> Split the dough, choose each other's strangest topping, and see whose pizza survives the taste test. It feels like an occasion without asking either of you to leave the kitchen.

Weak Why it fits:

> A great choice for couples who want a fun date.

Better Why it fits:

> You picked playful, under $30, and at home. This gives you something to laugh about, uses grocery-store ingredients, and wraps up in about 90 minutes.

## 6. Page-Level Copy

- **Hub:** promise a faster decision, then move directly into the Picker.
- **Picker questions:** use short spoken language; option labels must be scannable without their supporting copy.
- **Results:** lead with the recommendation, not an explanation of the algorithm.
- **Spokes and categories:** answer the search intent first, then help the user narrow it down. Do not pad pages to hit a word count.
- **Calls to action:** name the result of the action, such as "Find our three" or "Try another set," rather than generic "Learn more."

## 7. Editorial Review Checklist

A page or Idea cannot be published unless the editor can answer yes to all applicable checks:

- Does the opening create interest within the first two sentences?
- Can the reader picture what they will actually do?
- Are time, budget, setup, and constraints realistic?
- Does Why it fits reference real recommendation inputs?
- Has generic AI phrasing and repeated template language been removed?
- Does the copy add value beyond its research sources?
- Is every factual, safety, legal, or location-sensitive claim verified?
- Has the copy been read aloud and edited for natural rhythm?
- Do at least two relevant source IDs resolve to successful metadata-only research receipts?
- Does the Idea have a unique review ID, a research-use note, and a note explaining what the independent edit added?
- Does the catalog audit reject known repeated hook templates and duplicated Why-it-fits copy?
- Is this genuinely a date rather than a household chore, work break, errand, or abstract exercise?
- Can two people clearly share the activity and finish with a memorable couple-specific payoff?
- Does the image brief show the exact activity in progress with two adults, rather than a generic object or landscape?
